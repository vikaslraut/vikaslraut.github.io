Why this exists

Three things that make a practice exam worth sitting

01

It should match the real exam

The same screen, the same question states, the same rules about saving an answer, the same way sections open and close. Anything else trains students for an exam that does not exist.

It should use your papers

You already write and print excellent question papers. The system reads those directly, keeping your equations and diagrams exactly as they are.

03

It should survive exam day

Power cuts, a machine that dies in the ninetieth minute, a connection that drops for twenty seconds. This is where trust in a result is won or lost.

The save rule, reproduced exactly

In the real exam, choosing an option and then jumping to another question does not save it — only Save & Next does. It catches students out every year. Here they meet it in practice instead, and the palette shows them immediately.

A clock the computer cannot argue with

Time is kept centrally, so changing a machine’s clock changes nothing. The paper ends on its own when the time is up, exactly as the real exam does.

When something goes wrong

Answers do not depend on the connection holding

The whole paper is loaded onto each computer before the exam begins, and every answer is kept safe on the machine as well as centrally.

The network drops

Nothing happens on screen. The student keeps answering, and everything catches up when the connection returns.

A computer dies

The student moves to a spare seat, logs in, and continues from the same question with the correct time remaining.

The clock is kept centrally

Changing a machine’s time does nothing. Every student gets exactly the time the exam allows, and the paper ends by itself.

The power goes out

Work already done is safe. When the lab is back, the invigilator adds the lost time — to one student or to everyone.
